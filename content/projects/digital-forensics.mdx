My forensics experience comes from academic coursework during my MSc. It was not professional casework for a law enforcement body or a client.

## MSc forensic coursework

During my MSc in Cybersecurity and Digital Forensics at the University of Huddersfield, completed with Distinction in 2024, I used three established forensic tools in practical investigation exercises set as part of the course:

- **FTK Imager**, a tool for acquiring and previewing forensic images of storage media.
- **Autopsy**, an open-source platform for analysing disk images and the files, artefacts and activity within them.
- **EnCase**, a commercial forensic suite used for acquisition and analysis.

In these exercises I examined digital evidence, documented my observations as I worked and reported my findings. It taught me to separate what the evidence shows from what I might initially suspect.

These were academic exercises on prepared material. They were not criminal investigations.

## Why it matters for forensic and incident response roles

The coursework gave me the tools and the procedure: acquire carefully, record every step, keep evidence intact and report only what the evidence supports. Those habits carry over to incident response, where the first question is always the same: what actually happened?
