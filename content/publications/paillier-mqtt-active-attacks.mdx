## The problem

Encrypting sensor readings with Paillier protects their confidentiality, and it lets an authorised service add encrypted values without seeing them. It does not, by itself, stop an attacker from replaying old messages, injecting false ones or flooding the broker. The question is how a Paillier-protected smart-home system actually behaves when those attacks happen.

## Approach

The study uses my virtual smart-home testbed: simulated sensors publish Paillier-encrypted readings over MQTT, an authorised consumer processes them, and an isolated attacker machine carries out controlled attacks. Behaviour is observed through the system's own outputs, broker logs and network captures.

## Connection to my research

This paper evaluates the encrypted pipeline at the centre of my PhD under realistic attack conditions, rather than measuring the cryptography in isolation.
